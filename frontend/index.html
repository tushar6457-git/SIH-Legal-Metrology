    Legal Metrology (PCR) Rules 2011 — 2024-2026 Amendments Quick Reference

    The Department of Consumer Affairs has notified comprehensive amendments to prevent shrinkflation, ensure full pricing transparency, and protect consumers from misleading declarations.

    1. Unit Sale Price (USP) — Rule 6(1)(e) [2024 Amendment]

    Every packaged commodity must declare a Unit Sale Price (USP) printed prominently on the package alongside MRP:
    • If net quantity is above 1 kg / 1 Litre: declare price per kg or per Litre.
    • If net quantity is below 1 kg / 1 Litre: declare price per gram (g) or per millilitre (ml).
    Rounded to two decimal places. Non-declaration is punishable under Section 36(1).

    2. Minimum Numeral Height — Rule 9 & Schedule II

    3. Complete Address with PIN Code — Rule 6(1)(a)

    The address of manufacturer / packer / importer must state complete postal address including street/locality, city/district, state, and 6-digit Postal PIN Code. Mentioning mere city name is non-compliant and punishable under Section 36(1) of the Legal Metrology Act, 2009.

    4. Digital Consumer Care Details — Rule 6(1)(f)

    Consumer care information must include: Name/Designation of contact officer, complete postal address, toll-free / helpline telephone number, and a valid email ID. Omission of email is a violation as per 2024 Amendment.

    5. Country of Origin — Rule 6(1)(g) & 6(10)

    Mandatory for all packaged commodities (domestic and imported). For imported goods, the name and address of the Indian importer must also be declared on the physical package label. This declaration must be physically printed — hiding it only in a QR code is not acceptable.

    Enforcing Fair Trade, Accurate Measures & Consumer Trust

    The Legal Metrology Division operates under the Department of Consumer Affairs, Government of India. It exercises regulatory and statutory control over all weights, measures, weighing and measuring instruments, and packaged commodities manufactured, packed, imported, or distributed across the country.

    1. Statutory Framework: Legal Metrology Act, 2009

    The Legal Metrology Act, 2009 (Act No. 1 of 2010) was enacted by Parliament to establish and enforce standards of weights and measures, regulate trade and commerce in weights, measures, and other goods which are sold or distributed by weight, measure, or number, and for matters connected therewith. The primary objective is to safeguard consumers against short measurement, incorrect labeling, and deceptive packaging practices.

    2. Packaged Commodities Rules (PCR), 2011 & 2024–2026 Amendments

    Under Section 52(2)(j) and Section 18 of the Act, the Central Government framed the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011. These rules govern every pre-packaged commodity sold or exhibited for sale. The 2024–2026 amendments introduce stringent reforms:

    • Mandatory Unit Sale Price (USP): Rule 6(1)(e) requires every pre-packaged commodity to declare its price per standard unit (per kg/Litre or per g/ml) alongside the MRP to eliminate deceptive packaging and shrinkflation.
    • Standardized Numeral & Letter Height: Rule 9 (Schedule II) mandates minimum font heights based on Principal Display Area (PDA) to ensure consumers can clearly read all declarations without magnification.
    • Complete Postal PIN Code: Rule 6(1)(a) makes omission of the 6-digit PIN code an actionable offense under Section 36(1).
    • Digital Consumer Care Identity: Mandatory inclusion of telephone helpline, postal address, and active electronic mail address under Rule 6(1)(f).
    • Country of Origin on Physical Packaging: Rule 6(1)(g) and Rule 6(10) prohibit hiding origin declarations solely behind digital QR codes.
    3. Institutional Enforcement Hierarchy
    Director of Legal Metrology
    Central Government · Policy, Central Rules, Interstate Trade & National Standardization
    Controllers of Legal Metrology
    State / UT Governments · Enforcement Head in respective State jurisdictions
    Senior Inspectors & LMOs
    Field Enforcement · Market inspections, sampling, compounding, and prosecution
    4. Penalties & Offenses

    Violations of packaged commodity declarations attract penalties under Section 36(1) of the Legal Metrology Act, 2009 — punishable with fine up to ₹25,000 for first offense, up to ₹50,000 for second offense, and up to ₹1,00,000 or imprisonment for subsequent offenses.
